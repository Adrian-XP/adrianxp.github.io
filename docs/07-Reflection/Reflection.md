## Review of Module's Success

I was successfully able to have communication with all my teammates' subsystems, wirelessly communicate with the HMI subsystem, have several surface-mounted parts, and a switching regulator. Unfortunately, I failed to keep all my parts surface-mounted and ended up using a breakout board.


## Lessons Learned

1. Make sure footprints match the parts selected.
2. Try to start as early as possible with anything coding-related.
3. Have as many test points as possible.
4. Test team communication as early as possible.
5. Section everything that can fail so you don't have to pull everything out.
6. Do actual tests rather than taking the datasheet's word.
7. Document the success because you never know when it will stop working.
8. Relax, find people who can support you and snap you back to reality when needed.

## Recommendations for Future Students

Practice Beyond the Classroom
Engineering courses often fail to provide enough coding practice for true mastery. Spend time building projects on your own to sharpen your skills. Explore sites like GitHub to find endless ideas for new projects.

Own Your Code
Using AI can be helpful, but you must still understand every line you write. If you ask for help but cannot explain your own work, it frustrates others. Know your logic inside and out so you can troubleshoot effectively.

Verify All Technical Data
Never trust a datasheet or footprint blindly. These documents can contain errors just like any manual entry. Double-check your parts and custom materials to avoid costly mistakes in your design.

Plan for the Unexpected
This course requires you to look ahead without being told what is coming. If you wait until the last minute, your grade will likely drop. Start tasks early to stay ahead of the curve.

Prioritize Rest and Health
Working past 3:00 AM usually yields poor results. A lack of sleep ruins your focus the next day. Avoid relying on caffeine late at night, as it can quickly lead to a tough addiction.

Learn from Low-Stakes Failure
A bad grade might feel like a disaster now, but the classroom is a safe place to fail. In professional engineering, errors can lead to financial loss or physical danger. Take the time to learn things correctly now while the stakes are low.
